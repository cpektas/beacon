Beacon Releases

Four sessions running.One is waiting on you.

Beacon is a small status light for Claude Code. One dot per session, docked in the corner of your screen, coloured by what that session is actually doing. Click a dot to jump straight into it.

The colours

Four states, ordered by how much they want you. You learn them once and then you read the pill without thinking about it.

Needs you Blocked on a person — a question, or a permission it can't grant itself. Nothing moves until you answer.
Your move The turn finished. It's not stuck, it's done talking and waiting to hear what's next.
Working Busy, and the only dot that moves. Nothing is being asked of you.
Idle Open, quiet, nothing pending. Present so you can see it exists, not so you'll do anything about it.

What it does

The problem it solves is narrow: you started four things and you can only look at one of them.

It flashes when something waits

A brief full-screen pulse showing the same dots. It gives up after a few tries rather than nagging, and stays quiet when you're already looking at the session that wants you.

Position is identity

Dots sit in your sidebar's order and only ever change colour — never move. The second dot is the second session, every time you glance at it.

It says when one is safe to close

A finished session turns blue when the work landed and the tree is clean — a checkmark when that's certain, a question mark when it's a judgment worth a glance.

Setting it up

Three steps, about a minute.

  1. Drag Beacon to your Applications folder

    Open the disk image and drag it across, then launch it. There's no Dock icon — look for the pill in the corner of your screen.

  2. Install the status hooks

    Beacon's settings will offer to do this and explain what it changes. Without them every session reads as idle, because the hooks are what tell Beacon a turn started or finished.

    The hooks are Python, so this step needs python3 — macOS ships it with the Xcode Command Line Tools, which most people working with Claude Code already have.

  3. Grant Accessibility access

    Only needed so that clicking a dot can bring you to that session. Beacon works without it — you just lose the jumping.

Beacon reads files Claude Code already writes on your Mac and never modifies them. Nothing is sent anywhere: usage data stays local, and the setting that would share an anonymised copy is off unless you turn it on.

It updates itself — new versions arrive within a day, or immediately from Settings → Updates.
